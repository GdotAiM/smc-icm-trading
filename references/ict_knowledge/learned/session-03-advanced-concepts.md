### 3-step-guide-to-master-valid-pullback

Pullback in trading is a retracement of an impulse move, which can be a single candle or a number of candlesticks. Bearish candlesticks in a bullish move and bullish candlesticks in a bearish move are simply called pullbacks.

**Key Rules:**
1. Read the higher-timeframe context. 1-day and 4-hour charts — bullish, bearish or neutral structure
2. Identify the impulse leg. A clean directional move on the analysis timeframe
3. Mark the most extreme opposite-color candle. Bullish leg — the highest bullish candle. Bearish leg — the lowest bearish candle
4. Mark the GRAB level. Bullish — the low of the highest bullish candle. Bearish — the high of the lowest bearish candle
5. Wait for the pullback to grab the level. A wick is enough. The grab can be by a single candle or a cluster
6. Confirm the valid pullback is now valid. Both grab and break conditions are satisfied — the pullback is structurally complete
7. Use the valid pullback as inducement / order flow / order block. Wait for price to retrace back to the valid pullback zone

**When NOT to Use / Avoid:**
- you can jump to the section you are most interested in from below or continue reading the full article for a complete view.
- these are the recurring mistakes i see when traders first start identifying valid pullbacks.
- 1. **treating every retracement as a valid pullback.** a retracement is just a counter-color candle. a valid pullback requires both the grab and the break to occur in sequence.

### bear-flag-pattern-explained

The bear flag pattern is one of the most reliable bearish continuation patterns. Often seen in downtrends, it is formed when there is a strong sell-off followed by a period of consolidation. The objective of trading this pattern is to catch the next leg down in the trend.

**Key Rules:**
1. Confirm bearish trend. Higher-timeframe structure must be bearish — lower highs and lower lows
2. Identify the pole. An energetic bearish move with large-body candles and clear displacement
3. Mark the flag. Draw two trendlines connecting the highs and lows of the retracement — either upward-sloping or horizontal rectangle
4. Wait for the breakout. Price must close below the lower trendline of the flag
5. Wait for the retest. Price retraces back to the lower trendline after the breakout
6. Wait for confirmation. Bearish candlestick pattern, strong rejection wick, or lower-timeframe MSS at the retested trendline
7. Enter the sell. On the confirmation candle close

**When NOT to Use / Avoid:**
- you can jump to the section you are most interested in from below or continue reading the full article for a complete view.
- these are the recurring mistakes i see when traders first start trading bear flags.
- 1. **trading a bear flag against the trend.** the pattern only works in a bearish trend. a bear flag inside a bullish trend is just a bull retracement waiting to fail.
